With an electric water heater, the symptom is often a strong clue. No hot water anywhere suggests a tripped breaker, a tripped high-limit reset, or a failed upper element or upper thermostat, because the upper side controls when the lower side gets power. A few minutes of hot water followed by lukewarm points to the lower element or lower thermostat. Water that comes out scalding points to a thermostat stuck closed or an element shorted to the tank, which is also what trips the red reset button. The wiring gets inspected too, from the junction box to the thermostat terminals. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rust-colored hot water or moisture around the heater
  • Crackling noises as the heater runs, often from sediment
  • A short burst of hot water, then nothing but tepid
  • Cold water everywhere, even after waiting
  • A burning smell or a discolored access panel near the thermostat
  • A red reset button that keeps tripping
  • Water that comes out scalding hot
  • A circuit breaker for the heater that will not stay on

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Check the T&P relief valve annually, and call if it drips afterward
  • After any draining, refill the tank fully and run a hot faucet before restoring power
  • Get a diagnosis for any reset button that trips more than once
  • In hard water areas, a softener or low-watt-density elements can extend element life
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and operating cost
  • Flush the tank regularly to keep minerals from piling up around the lower element
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
