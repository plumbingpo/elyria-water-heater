How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Rust-colored water, leaks at fittings, or moisture under the heater
  • Energy costs climbing while usage stays flat
  • Fixtures that run too hot one minute and cool the next
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Test thermostatic mixing valves periodically to confirm safe delivery temperatures
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
  • Flush high-demand heaters on a regular interval to keep recovery strong
  • Check anodes on a schedule matched to your volume and water quality
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected

Should You DIY or Call a Pro?

A few tasks, like checking the temperature setting or lifting the relief valve lever to test it, are fine for homeowners, but most water heater work involves gas lines, venting, 240-volt circuits, or a tank full of scalding water, and that belongs with a professional.
