How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Age is the first clue. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. The symptoms come next. A leak from the tank itself, rusty water from the hot side only, and loud sediment noise all point to a tank near the end. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and homeowners in Elyria, OH usually find that out the hard way, with a wet floor.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. That is why Elyria Water Heater starts with a few questions: who showers when, whether there is a big soaking tub, and how many fixtures run at the same time. Tank heaters are compared by first-hour rating, while tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise needed in the coldest months. For a heat pump model, we check space and airflow, because it draws its heat from the surrounding room. Gas line, vent, and panel capacity are all confirmed ahead of time, so installation day holds no surprises.

The swap itself follows a checklist, not a guess: old tank drained and removed, fresh connectors and a working shutoff put in, gas lines leak-tested and fitted with a sediment trap, venting sized and pitched to the manufacturer's spec, and the relief valve piped to a safe discharge point. If local code requires a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps, they go in, and the permit is pulled whenever one is needed. Before we leave, we fill and purge the tank, confirm draft and check for carbon monoxide on gas units, set the temperature near 120 degrees, and walk you through the new heater. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
