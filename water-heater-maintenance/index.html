If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, causes that familiar rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Elyria, OH, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, pull and inspect the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Elyria

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

We arrive with the parts and tools the job usually needs, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Beyond the tools, experience matters: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge well before any of them cause trouble. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
