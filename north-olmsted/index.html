Keeping Your North Olmsted Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches North Olmsted Homeowners Face

In North Olmsted, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
